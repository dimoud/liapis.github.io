Building e-Identity and engineer’s certificate in Athens

Before a sale, a parental gift or a donation, the notary needs documents from an engineer. We tell you what your case requires and prepare it.

In short

Since 1/2/2021 every property transfer requires a Building e-Identity and a certificate of completeness, valid for two months from the site visit. That is what the notary means today by an “engineer’s certificate”.

Source: ν.5306/2026, ΦΕΚ Α΄ 88/8.6.2026, άρθρα 341 επ.

Updated: 18/11/2026

What the Building e-Identity is

It is the property’s electronic file on the Technical Chamber platform (Articles 341 ff., Spatial and Urban Planning Code, Law 5306/2026). Where they exist, it holds the building permit and its plans, legalised works, the Energy Performance Certificate, floor plans of the actual state and the completeness certificate.

It is required for property transfers, for permits for renovation or extensions, for programmes such as «Exoikonomo», for operating licences and for loans. For a flat, an e-Identity of the individual unit is prepared.

Until 2021 transfers relied on the engineer’s certificate of Article 83, Law 4495/2017. Since 1/2/2021 its place has been taken by the Building e-Identity with its certificate of completeness, valid for two months from the site visit. So when you are asked for “an engineer’s certificate for the contract”, this is what you need.

How we work

  1. We gather the permit, plans and legalisations — from the planning office if you do not have them.
  2. On-site inspection and survey.
  3. Comparison with the approved plans. If there is a difference, we tell you how to fix it before the contract.
  4. Entry on the Technical Chamber platform, completeness certificate and engineer’s certificate.

What we need from you

  • The title deed
  • The property’s Land Registry number (KAEK)
  • The building permit, if there is one
  • The Energy Performance Certificate and any earlier declarations of unauthorised works

If something is missing, we request it from the planning office.

How much the Building e-Identity costs

There is no fixed price, because the work is not the same for every property. Before you commit to anything, you know what your case needs, what it costs and how long it takes.

Frequently asked questions

The notary asks for an engineer’s certificate. Is that the same as the Building e-Identity?

In practice, yes. Since 2021 transfers are made with the Building e-Identity and its certificate of completeness, which replaced the old Article 83 certificate. The certificate is valid for two months from the site visit.

What happens if unauthorised works are found?

If the difference can be legalised, it is legalised first and the certificate of completeness is issued afterwards. That is why the check is best done before a contract date is fixed.

Is there an e-Identity for a flat too?

Yes. An individual unit gets its own e-Identity, with the details of the flat and of the building’s permit.

How much does the Building e-Identity cost for a flat?

It depends on whether there is a permit with drawings and whether the flat matches them. You send us the KAEK, the area and any documents you have, and you know the cost before you commit. If a legalisation or an EPC is needed, they are shown separately from the start.
