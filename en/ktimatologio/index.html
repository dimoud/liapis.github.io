Land Registry checks and corrections in Athens

A wrong area, shifted boundaries or a property shown as “unknown owner”. We check what the Land Registry says, what is true on the ground and which route leads to the correction.

In short

Area and boundaries are corrected by an application to the Land Registry, with a survey of changes and the consent of affected neighbours (article 19 §2, Law 2664/1998). We prepare the survey and our partner notary files the application.

Updated: 23/9/2026

What can be corrected

Geometric details such as area and boundaries are corrected by application to the Land Registry, with a survey of the change and the consent of any affected neighbours (Article 19(2), Law 2664/1998). The engineer does not file the application: our partner notary does, with the survey we prepare.

When the issue is ownership itself, for example the wrong owner or an “unknown owner” entry, the route is legal and the case proceeds with a lawyer. The deadline for correcting inaccurate first entries has been extended many times, so we check it for every case.

Either way, the starting point is an accurate measurement, which is why we begin with the topographic survey.

How we work

  1. We check the Land Registry extract and plan against your deeds.
  2. On-site measurement to show where the difference lies.
  3. We tell you whether it can be corrected by application or needs a court.
  4. Survey of the change and neighbours’ consent; our partner notary files the application.
  5. Follow-up until the entry is updated.

What we need from you

  • The title deeds and the surveys attached to them
  • The property’s Land Registry number (KAEK)
  • Contact details of the neighbours, if you know them
  • Any correspondence with the Land Registry

We request any missing extracts and plans from the Land Registry ourselves.

Frequently asked questions

The Land Registry shows a smaller area than my deed. Am I losing land?

Not necessarily. The difference often comes from how boundaries were drawn during the survey. We measure and tell you whether a correction is supported; if so, we prepare the survey and the application is filed through our partner notary.

Do I need my neighbour’s signature?

For a boundary correction that affects a neighbouring property, yes: the neighbour co-signs the application or gives a declaration of consent. If they refuse, it cannot be done by simple application.

Who files the correction application?

Our partner notary, so you do not need to find one yourself. We prepare the technical part and coordinate the process. When ownership itself is disputed, a lawyer is also involved.
